# scflow-ml: screening models for superconductivity and stability

Fast statistical screens that decide which structures are worth a full scflow (QE + EPW)
calculation. **They are not a substitute for that calculation.** Every number they print comes
with its cross-validated error and an applicability-domain check.

| Question | Model | Trained on |
|---|---|---|
| Is it a superconductor? | LightGBM classifier → P(SC \| tested) | 3DSC_MP: SuperCon entries matched to Materials Project structures |
| What Tc? | LightGBM regressor on log(1+Tc) | 3DSC_MP superconductors |
| Thermodynamically stable? (fast) | LightGBM regressor → E above hull | Materials Project, ~154k structures |
| Stable? (physics-based) | MACE-MP-0: relax at pressure → phonons (dynamic) + self-consistent convex hull (thermodynamic) | universal potential; hull from MP polymorphs re-relaxed at the same pressure |

## Setup

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv-ml && .venv-ml/bin/pip install -e '.[ml]'
echo "MP_API_KEY=<your key>" >> .env          # https://materialsproject.org/api — never commit .env
.venv-ml/bin/python ml/fetch_data.py         # ~5 min: 3DSC (15 MB) + Materials Project summary (~340 MB)
.venv-ml/bin/python ml/train.py sc           # superconductivity models + CV report
.venv-ml/bin/python ml/train.py stability    # E_hull model + CV report (featurizing 154k structures takes a while)
```

## Usage

```bash
.venv-ml/bin/python ml/predict.py my_structure.cif compounds/Nb_0GPa.yaml
.venv-ml/bin/python ml/mlip_stability.py compounds/CaH6_200GPa.yaml     # GPU recommended
```

## How it is evaluated, and why

Superconductor datasets are full of near-duplicates: one parent compound appears with dozens of
dopings, and the cuprates dominate the high-Tc tail. A random train/test split puts siblings on
both sides and makes models look much better than they are. Every metric below therefore comes
from **5-fold cross-validation grouped by chemical system** (of the undoped parent structure).
The random-split number is shown next to it so the leakage is visible.

## Results (5-fold CV, generated by `train.py`; see `models/*_report.json`)

### Superconductivity (3DSC_MP, 5773 entries, 1887 chemical-system groups)

| Model | Split | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | Tc MAE | median-baseline MAE | R² (log Tc) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all classes | random (leaky) | 0.887 | 0.938 | 3.2 K | 12.0 K | 0.86 |
| all classes | **grouped** | **0.792** | 0.884 | **6.6 K** | 12.0 K | 0.62 |
| conventional | random (leaky) | 0.887 | 0.944 | 1.5 K | 4.8 K | 0.77 |
| conventional | **grouped** | **0.832** | 0.910 | **3.4 K** | 4.8 K | 0.49 |
| conventional | grouped, ordered structures only | 0.835 | 0.849 | 1.9 K | 2.7 K | 0.32 |

How to read this:
- The random split overstates ROC-AUC by about 0.05–0.10 and understates the Tc error by about 2×.
  Quote the grouped numbers.
- The classifier is useful for ranking candidates. The Tc regressor beats a constant guess, but
  only modestly for conventional superconductors. Treat it as an order of magnitude.
- **A dataset artifact was found and removed.** 3DSC represents doped compounds as disordered
  supercells, and 81% of those are superconductors, against 52% of ordered entries. The first model
  ranked `is_ordered` as its most important feature, which encodes how the dataset was built, not
  the material. `is_ordered` and `nsites` are excluded from training.
- **Hydrides are missing.** Only 24 of 5773 entries contain hydrogen (mostly PdH), and none are
  high-pressure hydrides: Materials Project has no high-pressure structures. These models cannot
  speak to hydride superconductivity, and `predict.py` flags such inputs as out of domain. The
  validated scflow runs in the results database are the way to build that training set.

### Thermodynamic stability (Materials Project, 154,373 structures, 49,572 chemical-system groups)

| Split | E_hull MAE | median-baseline MAE | ROC-AUC (on hull) | ROC-AUC (within 0.1 eV/atom) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| random | 58 meV/atom | 128 meV/atom | 0.864 | 0.924 |
| **grouped** | **63 meV/atom** | 128 meV/atom | **0.867** | **0.911** |

A 63 meV/atom error is the size of the window that separates stable from metastable, so this model
is a coarse filter only. Use `mlip_stability.py` (MACE relaxation plus hull) for a sharper answer at
ambient pressure, and DFT for a final answer. (The grouped/random gap is small here because hull
distance depends on the competing phases, not on near-duplicate siblings.)

**The feature model cannot rank polymorphs.** All polymorphs of a composition share composition
features. bcc Nb (the ground state) and fcc Nb (+0.32 eV/atom) are both one-atom cubic cells, and
the model predicts 0.28 eV/atom for bcc Nb. The MACE hull handles this correctly:

| Structure | MP DFT E_hull | feature model | MACE-MP-0 hull (`mlip_stability.py`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nb (bcc) | 0 | 0.284 eV/atom | 0.000 eV/atom (4 reference phases, 19 s) |
| MgB₂ | 0 | 0.045 eV/atom | 0.003 eV/atom (15 reference phases, 46 s) |

### MACE-MP-0 phonons vs scflow DFPT (`validate_mlip.py`, same geometry and q-points)

| Structure | DFPT min / max (cm⁻¹) | MACE min / max (cm⁻¹) | Stability verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nb, 0 GPa | 10 / 219 | 0 / 155 | agrees (stable); MACE frequencies about 30% soft |
| NbN rock salt, 0 GPa | −320 / 591 | −143 / 390 | agrees (unstable at X): independent confirmation of the DFPT instability |
| CaH₆, H on wrong site (12e) | −3270 / 7419 | −55631 / 28648 | agrees (unstable) |
| CaH₆, correct (12d), 200 GPa | −4 / 2079 | −206180 / 87933 | **disagrees**: MACE-MP-0 breaks down at megabar compression |

The universal potential is a reasonable first screen for dynamic stability at ambient pressure,
but its frequencies are not accurate enough for Tc. It runs about 30% soft for Nb, and for MgB₂ the
E₂g mode that drives superconductivity comes out near 760 cm⁻¹, where DFT and Raman put it around
520–600 cm⁻¹. At 200 GPa its predictions are meaningless; there, scflow's
DFPT has to decide.

### Candidate shortlist for scflow (`screen.py`)

These are Materials Project compounds that are metallic (per MP), within 25 meV/atom of the hull,
have ≤ 12 atoms per cell, contain no radioactive elements, and are not in SuperCon, either as
listed or as the parent of a doped entry. They are ranked by P(SC) × Tc estimate. Top entries:

| MP id | Formula | Space group | Atoms | MP E_hull | Known compound? | P(SC) | Tc estimate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mp-1221519 | Mo₂CN | 25 | 4 | 0 | hypothetical | 0.91 | 11 K |
| mp-1216436 | V₆GaGe | 200 | 8 | 0.001 | hypothetical | 1.00 | 9 K |
| mp-4262 | BeAlB | 216 | 3 | 0 | yes (ICSD) | 0.52 | 18 K |
| mp-1228829 | AlV₆Ge | 200 | 8 | 0 | hypothetical | 1.00 | 9 K |
| mp-1220597 | Nb₃SnH₂ | 40 | 12 | 0.003 | hypothetical | 0.83 | 9 K |
| mp-12737 | YB₂Rh₂C | 139 | 6 | 0.005 | yes (ICSD) | 0.81 | 8 K |

How to read it:
- The list mostly contains ordered variants of families the model already knows: A15 V₃X
  ternaries, borides, carbides and nitrides. A statistical model interpolates; it will not
  propose a new class of superconductor.
- Some entries are artifacts of the input data. YbSc(BO₃)₂ appears because MP's GGA calls it
  metallic (a known f-electron problem); it is almost certainly an insulator.
- The useful next step is to run scflow on the experimentally known, cheap entries (BeAlB,
  3 atoms; YB₂Rh₂C) and feed the validated results back into the database.

## What the labels mean (read before using the classifier)

- SuperCon negatives (Tc = 0) are compounds someone measured and found not superconducting above
  their measurement temperature. Those compounds were chosen because they looked promising. The
  classifier therefore estimates **P(superconductor | it was worth testing)**. For an arbitrary
  Materials Project compound, the true prior is far lower.
- The `conventional` models exclude cuprates, iron-based and heavy-fermion superconductors. Those
  are the classes where electron–phonon theory (and so scflow) applies. Use those models when
  choosing what to send to scflow.
- Materials Project and SuperCon data are at ambient pressure. Megabar hydrides lie far outside
  the training domain; `predict.py` says so explicitly.
